What's real vs. estimated. Wage, occupation, worksite and dependent-employer status only exist for registrations that actually resulted in a filed I-129 petition — solid dots are that real, observed population. Everyone else who was submitted into the lottery (selected or not, we can't tell which without a filing) has no wage/occupation data at all, so outline dots estimate that remainder assuming it shares the same wage/occupation shape as the observed filers, scaled to match the year's real total submission count.

Median and average salary are computed only from the real filed population (no estimation), after excluding roughly 0.3–0.5% of records with implausible wage values (under $10K or over $1M/year, almost certainly data-entry errors) from the average specifically — the median is unaffected either way.

FY2021 and FY2022 are disabled — this FOIA release captured occupation, industry, worksite and dependent-employer status for those years' filed petitions, but never populated the wage field, so the grid's y-axis has nothing to plot. Would need a different source (e.g. DOL LCA data) to fill that gap.

Occupation categories are USCIS's own I-129 job-code groupings (not BLS/SOC). H-1B dependent is the employer's self-attested status on the petition (S1Q1A) — a legal ratio test, not a direct "staffing/outsourcing" label. Map shows real worksite states for the filed subset only, not extrapolated.
