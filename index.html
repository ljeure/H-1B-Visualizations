The rules this tool follows

• Who applies, what they do, and what they get paid is based on real 2024 government data (the most recent full data available). We assume today's and future applicants look similar, scaled to the 343,981 people who actually applied in 2026.

• If you drag the slider to change how many people apply, everyone's chances change by the same amount — the mix of jobs and salaries stays the same.

• Wage level weighted and top wage level directly sort people into four pay grades (based on how their salary compares to others in the same field and area) and give higher grades a better shot.

• Top salary skips randomness entirely — the highest-paid applicants get in first, full stop.

• The $100K fee assumes everyone applying from outside the U.S. drops out entirely, since they'd have to pay it to get a visa. People already in the U.S. changing status don't pay it, so they're unaffected.

• The $103K fee assumes everyone earning under $103K drops out entirely. For people earning more, you pick how much it thins out applications — whichever amount you pick, we assume the lowest earners above $103K are the first to drop out.

• The map and the "picked from H-1B dependent employers" stat use real, separately-measured 2024 data on which companies are H-1B dependent (a legal category for employers whose workforce leans heavily on H-1B visas) and where they're located — not an estimate from the salary data.
