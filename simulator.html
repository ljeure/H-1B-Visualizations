Base shape. Wage, occupation, and dependent-employer mix all come from FY2024's real filed-petition data (the only year with full microdata) — scaled to whatever total the slider picks. The default, 343,981, is FY2026's real total instead of FY2024's, since FY2026 was the last cap season before any of these policies took effect — so the mix is FY2024's, but the baseline size is FY2026's. That's a real seam, not a single clean year of data.

Wage-level quartiles are a proxy, not DOL's real methodology (which weights by occupation and metro area). Levels are fixed at FY2024's own wage quartiles: Level I <$82K, II $82–98K, III $98–126K, IV >$126K.

Weighted and level-based selection are expected-value proportional allocations across the fine wage distribution, not a discrete random draw of individuals.

$100K consular fee & dependent employers. The disproportionate effect is real, not assumed: in FY2024 data, 75.1% of H-1B-dependent employers' filings were consular-track vs. only 39.3% for non-dependent employers (computed directly from the I-129's own processing-type field). This scenario applies those two real rates directly — dependent employers retain 24.9% of their entries, non-dependent employers retain 60.7% — rather than a single average cut.

$103K fee. Below-$103K demand assumed to go to zero. Above $103K, the reduction percentage removes the lowest earners in that remaining pool first, computed at fine ($10K-bin) resolution even though the occupation chart displays coarser bins.

Map reweights FY2024's real worksite-state totals by this scenario's overall retention+selection rate for dependent vs. non-dependent employers — it does not re-derive geography from scratch.
